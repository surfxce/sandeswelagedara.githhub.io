Benny Cheesecake

November 2026 · 2.5 hr prep · 50 min bake · 8–12 serves

What could be better than a chocolate cheesecake? How about a pentuple chocolate cheesecake? Beautifully marbled throughout and slices cleanly. Yeah, enough said.

This is called Benny cheesecake after my dog Benny, who also has a double choc combination going on:

This recipe was adapted from the RecipeTinEats “Baked Cheesecake” recipe. The original is an incredible recipe I could not recommend enough.

Benny Cheesecake

Pentuple chocolate cheesecake with caramelized brown butter crust, five silky marbled chocolate batters, and a glossy tri-color ganache.

Prep Time 2.5 hrs
Cook Time 50 mins
Chill Time Overnight
Servings 8–12 serves
Approx Cost ~$40ish

Note on prep time: 2.5 hours (I’m not lying like every other recipe out there!). Servings: Roughly 8 serves, or 12 stingy ones. Don’t make me calculate nutrition.

Equipment Needed

19–20cm circular springform tin Balloon whisk 5 mixing bowls (or 2 for alternate method) Oven Blender / food processor (or ziplock & rolling pin) Kitchen scale

Ingredients

Crust

Cheesecake Filling

Tri-Chocolate Ganache Glaze (Optional)

Method 1: The Pentuple Marbled Method (Classic 5-Bowl)

Part 1: The Brown Butter Crust

  1. Preheat your oven to 180°C standard (or 160°C fan-forced).
  2. Blitz the biscuits: Blitz 250g of Chocolate Ripple biscuits (or biscuit of your choice) in a blender or food processor. You want a uniform, fine crumb that is not chunky, yet not ground completely into a powdery dust.
  3. Hydrate with brown butter: Add a tablespoon of your browned butter Note 1 and continue to blitz. Keep titrating (adding a tablespoon at a time) until the crumb mixture is just hydrated enough to look like wet sand, but not so much that it starts looking shiny or greasy. (Chocolate Ripple is quite a dry biscuit, so it needs a fair bit of butter).
  4. Press into the tin: Pour the crust mixture into your 19–20cm circular springform tin (greasing or lining with baking paper is up to your discretion). Use the flat bottom of a cup or glass to press firmly and evenly into the base and slightly up the sides.
  5. Blind bake: Bake the empty crust for 10 minutes Note 8 to set it and make it crisp.
  6. Cool: Pull the tin out of the oven and allow the crust to cool completely while you prepare the filling.

Part 2: The 5-Chocolate Fillings

  1. Bring cream cheese to room temperature: Ensure your 500g block cream cheese is truly at room temperature before starting (leaving it out on the counter for 2–3 hours is ideal).
  2. Cream the base batter: In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the cream cheese, sour cream, and sugar until completely smooth and homogenous. Whisk in the vanilla extract and the 2 tbsp plain flour.
  3. Incorporate eggs one by one: Add the 3 large eggs one at a time, fully whisking each egg into the batter before adding the next. Do not overbeat — we do not want to aerate the mixture; baked cheesecake should be dense, creamy, and velvety, not airy or spongy.
  4. Divide into 5 bowls: Divide the plain cheesecake batter evenly across 5 separate mixing bowls (roughly 200g per bowl, or ~190g allowing for residual bowl loss).
  5. Make the blonde chocolate: In a microwave-safe bowl, melt 80g of white chocolate in short bursts (start with 30–45s, then stir and continue in 5–15s increments). Keep stirring through the initial grainy stage until the chocolate turns a light golden caramel colour and smells toasty and nutty Note 11.
  6. Melt remaining chocolates: In separate microwave-safe bowls, melt the other batches of chocolate Note 10: 80g white chocolate, 80g milk chocolate, 80g dark chocolate, and 80g bittersweet chocolate. Microwave in 30-second intervals, stirring between bursts until smooth and runny.
  7. Mix each chocolate into its bowl:
    • Bowl 1: Fold in the melted blonde chocolate.
    • Bowl 2: Fold in the melted white chocolate.
    • Bowl 3: Fold in the melted milk chocolate.
    • Bowl 4: Fold in the melted dark chocolate + 1 teaspoon of ristretto.
    • Bowl 5: Fold in the melted bittersweet chocolate + 1.5 teaspoons of ristretto.
    Stir each bowl thoroughly until uniform. Either wipe your spatula cleanly between bowls or use 5 separate spoons to avoid muddling the colours.

Part 3: Putting It Together (Natural “Z” Marbling)

  1. First marbling pass: Take your cooled blind-baked crust. Pour half of the bittersweet chocolate batter in a bold “Z” zig-zag formation across the bottom.
  2. Rotate and layer for contrast: Rotate the cake tin roughly 70 degrees, and pour half of the white chocolate batter in a “Z” formation. Continue rotating ~70° and pouring half of each batter in this exact sequence:
    Dark → Blonde → Milk
    Then repeat with the second half of all batters in reverse contrast order:
    White → Bittersweet → Blonde → Dark → Milk
    This alternating high-contrast layering ensures a striking, marbled cross-section throughout the cake completely organically — you don’t even need a skewer or swirl stick!

Part 4: Baking & Chilling

  1. Bake: Bake in the oven at 160°C fan-forced (180°C conventional) for 50 minutes, or until the edges are gently set, the top has taken on a gorgeous golden colour, and the centre still has a slight, soft wobble/jiggle.
  2. Gradual cooling: Turn the oven off and prop the oven door slightly ajar to let the cheesecake cool slowly for an hour. Then bring it to room temperature on the countertop, and finally transfer to the fridge to chill overnight. (Chilling overnight lets the cheesecake set cleanly for knife cuts and ensures the ganache won’t melt upon application).

Part 5: Tri-Chocolate Ganache Glaze

  1. Warm the cream: In a small saucepan over medium-low heat, warm 100g of thickened cream until it just starts to steam. Do not let it boil.
  2. Divide chocolates: In 3 small heatproof bowls, place 35g of white chocolate in one, 35g of milk chocolate in the second, and 35g (or 25g) of bittersweet/dark chocolate in the third.
  3. Emulsify: Pour equal amounts of the hot cream into each bowl. Let sit for 1 minute so the chocolate melts, then stir each gently until completely glossy, smooth, and homogenous.
  4. Decorate: Artistically drizzle, flick, or pipe patterns across the top of your chilled cheesecake using all three chocolates — let your creativity take over! Return to the fridge for 20 minutes to set the glaze, slice with a hot dry knife, and serve.

Alternate Method: The 2-Bowl Layered Cheesecake

Only have 2 bowls to spare? No problem! You can get the exact same delicious cheesecake in a beautiful layered formation — it just takes a tiny bit of bowl-washing along the way.

Crust instructions are exactly the same as above.

  1. Cream the main batter: In your large bowl, cream the room-temperature cream cheese, sour cream, and sugar together until completely homogenous. Add vanilla extract and flour, then beat in the 3 eggs one by one until smooth. Do not overbeat!
  2. Weigh out portions: You will have roughly 1000g of batter total. Divided by 5 is 200g, but accounting for bowl residue, aim for roughly 190g of batter per chocolate layer.
  3. Layer 1 (Blonde): Take your second bowl (microwave-safe) and caramelize 80g of white chocolate in intervals until browned, golden, and toasty. Pour 190g of plain batter into it, whisk thoroughly, and pour evenly over the blind-baked crust.
  4. Wash & dry thoroughly: Wash and dry the second bowl meticulously. Make sure there is not a single water droplet left on the bowl or spatula — any rogue droplet of water will cause subsequent chocolate to seize up into an unusable grainy lump!
  5. Layer 2 (Bittersweet + Ristretto): Melt the 80g bittersweet chocolate in 30-second bursts. Stir in 190g of batter plus 1 tsp of ristretto. Gently pour in an even layer directly over the blonde base.
  6. Layers 3, 4 & 5: Repeat the melt-and-mix process in the washed and dried bowl for the remaining chocolates:
    • Milk chocolate: Melt 80g, mix with 190g batter, pour layer.
    • White chocolate: Melt 80g, mix with 190g batter, pour layer.
    • Dark chocolate: Melt 80g, mix with 190g batter (without ristretto), pour final top layer.
  7. Bake: Bake at 160°C fan (180°C standard) for 50 minutes until the top is golden and the centre has a gentle jiggle.
  8. Cool: You can choose to cool it slowly with the oven door ajar, but if that’s too much faff, you can chuck the whole 1kg mass at 160°C straight into your mini fridge. Who cares about food safety and everything else melting!
  9. Glaze: Finish with the tri-chocolate ganache glaze as detailed in Method 1.

Recipe Notes & Chef Tips

1. Browning the Butter ↑ back to ingredients

Melt 120 grams of butter over medium heat, and stir constantly until it starts to darken, foam, and smell nutty and caramelly. Immediately take off the heat and keep stirring to help cool it down and prevent burning.

2. Vanilla Bean Extract ↑ back to ingredients

I prefer to use vanilla bean extract with real seeds since it lends itself to a much more whole and complex vanilla flavour, but imitation and regular extract are completely fine.

3. White & Blonde Chocolate ↑ back to ingredients

I used Whittaker’s 28% Cocoa white chocolate, but any good quality white chocolate works. If you buy commercial blonde chocolate (like Cadbury’s Caramilk) you save the step of needing to caramelise the white chocolate yourself — simply use 80g white chocolate and 80g blonde chocolate, and skip the caramelising steps.

4. Milk Chocolate ↑ back to ingredients

I used Whittaker’s 33% milk chocolate, but any milk chocolate should be fine. Avoid Lindor blocks or truffles since the filling isn’t pure chocolate and may interfere with how the cheesecake bakes and sets. Use only solid eating chocolate.

5. Dark Chocolate ↑ back to ingredients

Dark chocolate is between 50–70% cocoa, so I used Whittaker’s 50% dark chocolate here for balance.

6. Bittersweet Chocolate ↑ back to ingredients

Bittersweet is defined as over 70% cocoa, so I used Whittaker’s 72% Dark Ghana here for deep chocolate punch.

7. Ristretto & Coffee Substitutions ↑ back to ingredients

Ristretto is a shorter, more concentrated version of espresso. Essentially, you pull a regular espresso shot with less liquid (~15–20ml). If you have a Nespresso machine, pull an espresso shot but stop it short halfway through. If you have a manual espresso machine, cut the shot short. If you only have instant coffee, dissolve 1 teaspoon of granules into 20mL of warm water (approx. 1 tablespoon).

8. Blind Baking ↑ back to ingredients

Blind baking is simply baking the biscuit crust on its own before adding any filling. This helps set the butter and biscuits so the crust stays crunchy and doesn’t get soggy once the batter is poured in.

9. Sour Cream Confession ↑ back to ingredients

I made it without sour cream… because I forgot to add it… will update soon! (Cheesecake still turned out incredible, but sour cream adds that quintessential tangy balance).

10. Microwave Melting Technique ↑ back to ingredients

I used 30-second intervals at medium power, but if you have a higher power microwave use 15-second bursts. It’s better to be cautious, as overheating chocolate can cause it to seize — turning into a weird, grainy, separated lump that has no business in this cheesecake!

11. Caramelising White Chocolate (Blonde Chocolate) ↑ back to ingredients

Blonde chocolate takes patience. I initially used 30-second intervals until I realised I was simply remelting the same white chocolate with no caramelisation occurring. I upped it to 45 seconds, and as soon as I mixed I saw beautiful golden streaks of chocolate. When making blonde chocolate it will turn into a weird grainy lump at the start — don’t panic, keep stirring and it will turn runny and silky again. As soon as it darkens to a light tan/amber colour, stop. The more you blond it, the higher the risk it burns or seizes into an unusable mass.
