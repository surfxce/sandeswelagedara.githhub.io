Benny Cheesecake

November 2026 · 2.5 hr prep · 50 min bake · 8–12 serves

What could be better than a chocolate cheesecake? How about a pentuple chocolate cheesecake? Beautifully marbled throughout and slices cleanly. Yeah, enough said.

This is called Benny cheesecake after my dog Benny, who also has a double choc combination going on:

This recipe was adapted from the RecipeTinEats “Baked Cheesecake” recipe. The original is an incredible recipe I could not recommend enough.

Prep time: 2.5 hours (I’m not lying like every other recipe out there!). Cook time: 50 minutes

Approx cost: $40ish. As of November 2026.

Roughly 8 serves, or 12 stingy ones. Don’t make me calculate nutrition.

IngredientsMethod2-bowl methodNotesPhotos

For this recipe, you will need:

Crust

  • 250g Chocolate Ripple Biscuits (or Marie)
  • 100g of melted butter, browned and caramelised (note 1)

Glaze (optional)

  • 100g thickened cream
  • 35g bittersweet chocolate
  • 35g white chocolate
  • 35g milk chocolate

Filling

  • 3 large eggs
  • 500g of cream cheese (in block form, at room temperature)
  • 2 tbsp plain flour
  • 1 cup white sugar
  • 1/2 cup sour cream (note 9)
  • 1.5–2 tsp vanilla/vanilla bean extract (note 2)
  • 160g white chocolate (note 3)
  • 80g milk chocolate (note 4)
  • 80g dark chocolate (note 5)
  • 80g bittersweet chocolate (note 6)
  • 1 shot of ristretto (note 7)

Equipment

  • 19–20cm circular springform tray
  • Whisk
  • A few mixing bowls
  • Oven
  • Blender/food processor (or ziplock bag and a rolling pin)
  • Scale (alternate method)

Crust

  1. Preheat oven to 180/160 fan-forced.
  2. Blitz 250g of Chocolate Ripple (or other biscuit of your choice) in a blender or food processor. Enough so that it is uniform and not chunky, yet not so much so it turns into a fine powder.
  3. Add a tablespoon of the brown butter and continue to blitz, titrating (or keep adding a tablespoon) until it is just hydrated enough that it looks like wet sand, but not so much so it starts to look shiny or greasy. (I had to add quite a bit of butter to Chocolate Ripple, it’s a pretty dry cookie.)
  4. Pour the crust mixture into the tin (I don’t usually oil or grease the pan, nor use baking paper, but it is up to your discretion) and use the bottom of a flat cup to press into the bottom and onto the sides, making sure the crust is uniform throughout.
  5. Blind bake the crust for 10 minutes (note 8).
  6. Pull out of the oven and allow it to cool.

Filling

  1. Ensure your cream cheese is at room temperature, leaving it out for 2–3 hours would be fine.
  2. In a large bowl, cream the cream cheese, sour cream and the sugar together, making sure they are homogeneous — utilising a whisk. Then add the vanilla extract and flour. Then add the 3 eggs, one by one and incorporate each one fully before adding the next. Don’t overbeat as to aerate the batter, cheesecake is not airy or spongy.
  3. Divide equally into 5 different bowls. (Alternate method will have 1-bowl method.)
  4. Melt 80g in a microwave, using 45-second increments. Keep microwaving until the chocolate turns a light golden colour and smells caramelly. This is blonde chocolate. (note 11)
  5. Melt 80g of white chocolate, 80g of bittersweet chocolate, 80g of dark chocolate and 80g of milk chocolate in separate batches. One method is to microwave for 30 seconds at a time, and then mix until it is runny. (note 10)
  6. To the first bowl, add the blonde chocolate. To the second, add the white chocolate. To the third, add the milk chocolate. To the fourth add the dark chocolate. To the 5th add the bittersweet and 1 teaspoon of ristretto.
  7. Mix each batch separately, preferring to either wipe your spoon in between batches or using 5 separate spoons.

Putting it together

  1. Get the blind-baked crust and pour half the bittersweet chocolate batter in a “Z” formation.
  2. Rotate the cake 70 degrees and pour half the white chocolate batter in a Z formation. Continue to do this for all the batters in this order: dark, blonde, milk. Then with the other half of the batter, in this order: white, bittersweet, blonde, dark, milk. This ensures striking contrast. This also means you don’t need to use a skewer or other object to marble, it is already marbled.
  3. Put it in the oven for 50 minutes or until the top is a beautiful golden colour and the centre is still jiggly.
  4. Keep the oven door open to allow it to cool in the oven, then cool on a countertop before allowing to chill in the fridge overnight. This allows the ganache to not melt.

Ganache

  1. Warm 100g of thickened cream over the stove until starting to steam.
  2. In 3 heatproof bowls, add 35g of white chocolate to one, 35g of milk chocolate to another and 35 grams of bittersweet chocolate to the last one.
  3. Add equal amounts of warmed thickened cream to each, and stir each one until the chocolate is fully incorporated.
  4. Artistically create a pattern on the top using all three, up to you what it is!

Alternate method (2-bowl)

Only have 2 bowls to spare? No problem! You can get the same delicious cheesecake in a beautiful layered formation, just might take a little longer.

Crust instructions are exactly the same.

Filling

  1. Ensure your cream cheese is at room temperature, leaving it out for 2–3 hours would be fine.
  2. In a large bowl, cream the cream cheese, sour cream and the sugar together, making sure they are homogeneous — utilising a whisk. Then add the vanilla extract and flour. Then add the 3 eggs, one by one and incorporate each one fully before adding the next. Don’t overbeat as to aerate the batter, cheesecake is not airy or spongy.
  3. You should have roughly 1000g of batter. Divided by 5 is 200, but a lot gets spilt or left in the bowl, so let’s say 190g per chocolate.
  4. Get your one other bowl (microwave-safe) and make the blonde chocolate in it, microwaving it in intervals until it browns and smells nice and toasty. Then pour 190g of plain batter into it. Mix thoroughly and add to the crust you’ve blind baked.
  5. Wash and dry thoroughly, making sure there’s not a single water droplet on the bowl or any mixing utensil. Water will cause chocolate to seize and it will be good for nothing.
  6. Melt the bittersweet chocolate (microwave in 30-second intervals) and add 190g of batter, plus 1 tsp of ristretto. Then pour in 1 even layer on top of the blonde chocolate. Keep doing this for milk, white and dark (without the ristretto).
  7. Put it in the oven for 50 minutes or until the top is a beautiful golden colour and the centre is still jiggly.
  8. You can choose to cool it with the oven door open, but I find that’s such a faff and just chuck the whole 1kg mass at 160 degrees Celsius in my mini fridge. Who cares about food safety and everything else melting!

Ganache

  1. Warm 100g of thickened cream over the stove until starting to steam.
  2. In 3 heatproof bowls, add 35g of white chocolate to one, 35g of milk chocolate to another and 35 grams of bittersweet chocolate to the last one.
  3. Add equal amounts of warmed thickened cream to each, and stir each one until the chocolate is fully incorporated.
  4. Artistically create a pattern on the top using all three, up to you what it is!

Notes

  1. Melt 120 grams of butter over medium heat, and stir constantly until it starts to darken and smell of caramel. Immediately take off the heat and keep stirring to help cool it down.
  2. I prefer to use vanilla bean extract with seeds since it lends itself to a much more whole and complex vanilla flavour, but imitation and regular extract are fine.
  3. I used Whittaker’s 28% cocoa white chocolate, but any white chocolate should work. If you also buy blonde chocolate (like Cadbury’s Caramilk) you save the step of needing to caramelise the white chocolate, hence use only 80g white chocolate and 80g blonde chocolate, and skip the steps to caramelise the white chocolate.
  4. I used Whittaker’s 33% milk chocolate but any milk chocolate should be fine. I would avoid the Lindor blocks/truffles since the filling isn’t pure chocolate and may interfere with how the cheesecake bakes and sets. Only solid chocolate.
  5. Dark chocolate is between 50–70% cocoa, so I used Whittaker’s 50% dark chocolate here.
  6. Bittersweet is defined as over 70% cocoa, so I used Whittaker’s 72% Dark Ghana.
  7. Ristretto is a more concentrated version of espresso. Essentially, you pull a regular espresso shot but with less liquid. If you have a Nespresso machine, pull an espresso but stop it short. If you have a regular espresso machine, pull a shot but cut it short. If you only have instant coffee, mix 1 teaspoon into 20mL of water/a tablespoon.
  8. Blind baking is simply baking the crust without any filling. This helps set the crust and make it more crispy.
  9. One time I made it without sour cream…because I forgot to add it… It’s still delicious so it is not a dealbreaker, but the water from the sour cream makes the cake bake more lightly and less dense. Without it, it’s a deliciously fudgy, dense, melt-in-the-mouth truffle cake. Still yum, but also lacks the tanginess you’d come to expect from cheesecake.
  10. I used 30-second intervals, but if you have a higher power microwave use 15. It’s better to be cautious as overheating the chocolate can cause it to seize and it won’t melt but rather clump into a weird grainy lump that has no business in this cheesecake!
  11. Blonde chocolate takes a while to make, so be patient. I initially used 30-second intervals until I realised I was simply remelting the same white chocolate, with no caramelisation occurring. I upped to 45 seconds, and as soon as I mixed I saw these beautiful golden chunks of chocolate. When making blonde chocolate it will be a weird grainy lump at the start, but keep mixing and it turns runny again. As soon as it starts to darken or tan slightly, stop. The more you keep blonding the greater the chance it burns or completely seizes up and becomes completely unusable.

And now you have a Benny cheesecake!
