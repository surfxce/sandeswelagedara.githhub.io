My Half-Hearted Coles Boycott

August 2026 · 3 min read

For a long time we've known how the Coles and Woolworths duopoly have been hurting Australian households. They've both been significant contributors to Australia's cost-of-living crisis, consistently nickel and dime farmers, and bit by bit they dehumanise the checkout process, starting with those automated checkout gates that keep you penned in like a farm animal. And who knows how it will end, with Coles' partnership with Palantir, whose software is less-than-holy.

So with this at the back of my mind, my "challenge" was to spend as little money at Coles as possible. Of course, I didn't fully commit to the challenge, it was just a little running joke in my mind; if I wanted to fully commit there's 1000 other ways I could've. Like by shopping at Aldi. But from this mini-challenge came 3 scenarios, and hopefully at the end I'd have persuaded you to shop local.

1. Avocados

The shopping center near where I live has a fruit and veg market. That's not unheard of, but what is unheard of are these massive avocados. They're genuinely so large, easily double those you can find at Coles. But here's the thing: they're perfectly ripe from the beginning. You can eat them immediately, or put them in the fridge to have a perfectly ripe avocado on demand. The ones at the big supermarket are never fully ripe, so it's a game of either waiting for them to ripen, or dealing with the hard, unripe flesh. I will admit they are double the cost, but at double the size it pays off. Plus the convenience of being ripe then and there makes it a better value proposition.

2. Minced beef

I've disliked cooking with minced beef/pork for a while. Every time I try cooking with it there's just an excess of water in the pan, and I'm left with some grey soupy mess that is not appetising, and half of what I started with. I decided to try mince from the butcher, just on a whim. Infinitely better. I was genuinely so surprised to see that as it cooked it barely shrunk. And I wasn't left with some liquid mess at the end. On top of this, there was simply more meat left. Again is it more expensive? Yes, but I haven't heard of anyone wanting to pay for water as part of their minced meat. Incredible value proposition, I will not be going back.

3. Assorted international snacks

Asian snacks like Pocky, Pepero and YamYam have become popular enough for Coles to start stocking them. But your local Asian grocer will also stock them. This time this isn't a question about price or size. It's a question about customer service.

When I check out at Coles, the only question I'm asked is "unexpected item in bagging area"… and that's not a question. When I was buying snacks for my netball team, I was asked an array of warm questions. Finally! Real human interaction! He asked what they were for, as well as how many people were on my team, where we play and how many are on the court at once. Very basic questions. But questions that would never be asked if you went to Woolworths.

Just that interaction alone has made it so that I want to make a conscious, deliberate effort to buy my snacks from this store. That and 10% off for students! Win win.

So, in short

Why should you stop shopping at the big supermarkets?

  1. Better quality food can be found elsewhere.
  2. It's a better value, even though it might not look like it at first.
  3. Customer service is unparalleled. And you get to engage with your community a little more, over a national franchise.

I've been enjoying better quality food and service as a result of this switch, and I want better for all of you.
